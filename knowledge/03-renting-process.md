# Renting a Property in Dubai: Process, Costs and Documents

## Steps to rent a property in Dubai
Renting a home in Dubai typically follows these steps:

1. Set your annual budget and choose your areas.
2. View properties and choose one.
3. Agree the rent and terms with the landlord through an agent.
4. Sign the tenancy contract.
5. Pay the security deposit, the agency fee and the rent cheques.
6. Register the contract on Ejari.
7. Connect your electricity and water (DEWA) and collect the keys.

## Tenancy contract and Ejari registration in Dubai
A tenancy contract in Dubai sets out the rent, the length of the tenancy (typically one year, renewable), the payment schedule and the deposit. Ejari is the Dubai Land Department system for registering tenancy contracts. Registration is required, and without it the contract has limited legal protection, for example in a dispute. It is typically completed within 30 days of signing and is usually handled by the agent. The Ejari fee is typically around AED 220.

## Costs when renting in Dubai
Tenants typically pay these costs in addition to the first rent payment:

* Security deposit: typically 5% of the annual rent for unfurnished properties and 10% for furnished ones. It is refundable at the end of the tenancy, less any deductions for damage.
* Agency fee: at Marina Crest, the tenant pays 5% of the annual rent.
* Ejari registration: typically around AED 220.
* DEWA deposit: typically around AED 2,000 for an apartment and AED 4,000 for a villa, plus a small activation fee.
* Housing fee: typically 5% of the annual rent, usually billed monthly through the DEWA bill.

Some buildings also charge district cooling fees, which may not be included in the advertised rent.

## How rent is paid in Dubai
Rent in Dubai is traditionally paid by post-dated cheques handed over when the contract is signed. Landlords typically accept anywhere from 1 to 12 cheques, and 1, 2, 4 or 6 cheques are common. A smaller number of cheques can sometimes help you negotiate a lower rent, while more cheques may come with a slightly higher rent. The cheques are typically drawn on a UAE bank account.

## Documents tenants typically need in Dubai
Tenants typically provide a copy of their passport, their UAE residence visa and their Emirates ID. Some landlords also ask for proof of income, such as a salary certificate. People who have just arrived in the UAE may be asked for different documents, depending on their visa status. The agent will confirm the exact list for your case.

## Renewing a tenancy and rent increases in Dubai
Dubai limits how much a landlord can increase rent when a tenancy is renewed, using the RERA rental index, which tenants can check through official Dubai Land Department channels. A landlord typically must give at least 90 days' written notice before the tenancy ends if they want to increase the rent. Notice periods for ending a tenancy depend on the contract and the law. ## How long renting a property in Dubai typically takes
After you choose a property, renting typically takes around 5 to 10 days, including signing the contract, paying the deposit and cheques, registering Ejari (often 1 to 3 days) and receiving the keys. This is only an example, and it can be quicker or slower depending on the landlord.
