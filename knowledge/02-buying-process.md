# Buying a Property in Dubai: Process, Costs and Documents

## Who can buy property in Dubai
Foreigners, including people who do not live in the UAE, can typically buy property in Dubai in designated freehold areas. The six areas Marina Crest covers (Dubai Marina, JLT, Downtown Dubai, Business Bay, JVC and Palm Jumeirah) are generally freehold, but an agent should confirm the status of a specific property before you commit. Residents and non-residents can both buy, although non-residents often face stricter mortgage terms.

## Steps to buy a resale property in Dubai
Buying a ready, resale property in Dubai typically follows five steps:

1. Set your budget and decide whether to pay cash or use a mortgage.
2. View properties and agree a price with the seller.
3. Sign the sale agreement, called Form F, and pay a deposit.
4. The seller obtains a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the developer.
5. Buyer and seller meet at a Dubai Land Department registration trustee office, pay the balance and fees, and the title deed is issued in the buyer's name.

## Form F and the deposit when buying in Dubai
Form F is the standard resale sale agreement, also called the MOU. It records the price, the deposit, who pays which fees, any mortgage condition and the target transfer date. The deposit is typically 10% of the price, usually paid by cheque and held until transfer, often by the brokerage handling the sale. A lower deposit is sometimes agreed. The brokerage usually prepares the form, so read it carefully before signing.

## Costs on top of the price when buying in Dubai
Buyers typically pay these costs in addition to the purchase price:

* Dubai Land Department transfer fee: typically 4% of the price, plus a small administration fee.
* Agency commission: at Marina Crest, 2% of the agreed price on resale purchases.
* Registration trustee fee: typically around AED 2,100 for properties under AED 500,000 and AED 4,200 for higher values, including VAT.
* Title deed and administration fees: typically a few hundred dirhams.

The developer's NOC fee typically ranges from AED 500 to AED 5,000 and is usually paid by the seller. In total, extra costs for a cash buyer are often around 6% to 7% of the price. Mortgage costs come on top.

## Paying cash or taking a mortgage in Dubai
Paying cash is simpler and usually faster. With a mortgage, UAE Central Bank rules typically allow expat residents to borrow up to 80% of the price for a first property under AED 5 million, which means a 20% down payment. The limit is lower for more expensive properties and for second properties. Non-residents typically need a larger down payment, often around 40% to 50%, depending on the bank. Mortgage costs typically include a Land Department registration fee of 0.25% of the loan plus a small fee, a bank arrangement fee of around 0.5% to 1%, and a valuation fee of around AED 2,500 to AED 3,500. A mortgage pre-approval is a bank's indication of how much it may lend, not a final approval. Marina Crest introduces independent mortgage advisors but does not approve mortgages.

## Documents buyers typically need in Dubai
Buyers typically need a valid passport. Residents also provide their Emirates ID and residence visa copy. Mortgage buyers need a pre-approval letter or proof of funds. On transfer day the buyer and seller typically bring original identification, and the buyer brings payment for the balance and fees, commonly as manager's cheques. The seller provides the title deed and the developer's NOC. The agent will confirm the exact list for your case.

## How long buying a resale property in Dubai typically takes
For example, a cash purchase of a ready property often takes around 10 to 14 days from signing Form F to transfer. A mortgage purchase often takes around 4 to 6 weeks, because the bank must value the property and approve the loan. The developer's NOC typically takes around 5 to 10 business days. The title deed is typically issued at the trustee office on transfer day.

## Common mistakes when buying property in Dubai
Common mistakes include:

* not checking that the property is in a freehold area;
* not budgeting for the extra costs, which are often 6% to 7% of the price;
* signing Form F without understanding the mortgage condition;
* not confirming that the seller has cleared any outstanding mortgage or service charges before transfer;
* relying on verbal promises instead of written terms.
