# Living in Jumeirah Lakes Towers (JLT): Area Guide

## Overview of Jumeirah Lakes Towers (JLT)
Jumeirah Lakes Towers, usually called JLT, is a district of residential and commercial towers arranged around artificial lakes, next to Dubai Marina along Sheikh Zayed Road. The towers are grouped into 26 clusters, each identified by a letter, such as Cluster A or Cluster X. JLT is also a business district that is home to many companies, so residents include people who work nearby. JLT is one of the six areas Marina Crest Properties covers.

## Property types and sizes in JLT
Property in JLT is almost entirely apartments in mid-rise and high-rise towers. Typical sizes are around 350 to 550 sq ft for a studio, 650 to 1,000 sq ft for a one-bedroom, and 1,000 to 1,500 sq ft for a two-bedroom. Quality, layout and views vary a lot between clusters and between older and newer buildings, so two units of the same size can differ in price.

## Illustrative buying prices in JLT
Prices in JLT typically average around AED 1,300 to AED 1,900 per sq ft, and they vary widely by cluster, floor and view, with older towers lower. For example, a studio typically costs around AED 600,000 to AED 950,000, a one-bedroom around AED 900,000 to AED 1.5 million, and a two-bedroom around AED 1.4 million to AED 2.4 million. These figures are illustrative and change with the market, the building and the view.

## Illustrative rents in JLT
Annual rents in JLT typically depend on the cluster, the view and whether the unit is furnished. For example, a studio typically rents for around AED 55,000 to AED 75,000 a year, a one-bedroom around AED 80,000 to AED 120,000, and a two-bedroom around AED 120,000 to AED 180,000. These figures are illustrative and change with the market.

## Lifestyle in JLT
JLT has a community feel built around its lakes, with walking and jogging paths, parks, and cafés, restaurants and shops at the base of many towers. It is generally quieter than the busiest parts of Dubai Marina's promenade, while Dubai Marina and JBR are close by. JBR Beach is typically around 10 to 15 minutes away by tram. Many residents work in the offices nearby.

## Getting around JLT: metro, tram, parking and commute times
JLT is served by the DMCC station on the Dubai Metro Red Line, which links by a walkway to the Dubai Tram's Jumeirah Lakes Towers station. The tram connects to Dubai Marina, JBR and Dubai Media City. Walking distance to the metro varies a lot by cluster, so ask how far a specific tower is. Most towers have their own parking, but visitor parking can be limited. Outside peak hours, driving typically takes around 20 to 25 minutes to Downtown Dubai and DIFC, and around 25 to 35 minutes to Dubai International Airport. Peak-hour traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road can make journeys much longer.

## Service charges and building amenities in JLT
Service charges in JLT typically run around AED 13 to AED 20 per sq ft per year, depending on the age of the tower, its amenities and its cooling arrangement. They cover building maintenance, security and shared facilities. Many towers offer amenities such as a swimming pool, gym and concierge, but these vary by building. Ask an agent for the current service charge of a specific tower.

## JLT compared with Dubai Marina
Compared with Dubai Marina, JLT typically has lower purchase prices and rents. Dubai Marina typically averages around AED 2,000 to AED 2,600 per sq ft, while JLT typically averages around AED 1,300 to AED 1,900. JLT's atmosphere is more residential and business-oriented around the lakes, while Dubai Marina has a busier waterfront promenade. Dubai Marina is closer to the beach, with JBR Beach around 5 to 10 minutes on foot from most towers, while JLT residents typically use the tram or a short drive. Service charges are typically similar, and slightly lower in JLT. Neither area is better for everyone, and it depends on budget and lifestyle.

## Pros, cons and who JLT typically suits
JLT's pros are typically lower prices and rents than nearby waterfront areas, a lakeside setting, a wide choice of towers and good metro and tram access. Its cons are that building quality varies by cluster, walking distance to the metro varies, visitor parking can be limited, and it is not directly on the beach. It typically suits professionals who work nearby, couples and investors looking for lower entry prices, and it is less typical for people who want a beachfront home or a large garden.
