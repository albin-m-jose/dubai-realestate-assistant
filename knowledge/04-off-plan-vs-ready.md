# Off-Plan vs Ready Property in Dubai

## What off-plan and ready property mean in Dubai
Off-plan property in Dubai is a home sold before or during construction, usually directly by the developer. You buy from plans and pay in stages until the building is completed and handed over. Ready property, also called resale or secondary-market property, is a completed home that you can usually inspect, move into or rent out straight away. It is typically sold by an existing owner. Marina Crest Properties sells both.

## Payment structure: off-plan compared with ready property
Off-plan property is typically paid for in instalments linked to a payment plan, with a smaller amount due at the start. Ready property is typically paid in full at transfer, either in cash or with a mortgage, after an agreed deposit (typically 10%) is paid when the sale agreement is signed. Because off-plan payments are spread out, the money needed at the start is often lower than for ready property.

## Illustrative off-plan payment plans in Dubai
Off-plan payment plans differ between developers and projects, so no plan is standard across Dubai. For example:

* 60/40 plan: 60% is paid during construction and 40% at handover.
* 80/20 plan: 80% is paid during construction and 20% at handover.
* Post-handover plan: a smaller share is paid during construction and the rest is spread over one to five years after handover, depending on the developer.

Always read the developer's payment schedule carefully before signing.

## Registration and escrow for off-plan property in Dubai
Dubai law requires developers selling off-plan to open a project escrow account, and buyers' payments go into that account rather than to the developer's operating account. Off-plan sales are registered with the Dubai Land Department through a system called Oqood, which records your purchase while the building is unfinished. When the building is completed, the registration is converted to a title deed. Buyers should confirm that the project is registered and that payments go to the escrow account named in the contract.

## Mortgages: off-plan compared with ready property in Dubai
For ready property, UAE Central Bank rules typically allow expat residents to borrow up to 80% of the price on a first home under AED 5 million. For off-plan property, the Central Bank sets a lower maximum loan of 50% of the value, and individual banks may apply stricter conditions, such as requiring the project to be partly complete. Many off-plan buyers plan to arrange a mortgage for the final handover payment. Marina Crest does not approve mortgages and can introduce independent mortgage advisors.

## Costs and fees: off-plan compared with ready property in Dubai
For both types of property, the Dubai Land Department charges a registration fee, typically 4% of the price, plus small administration and title deed fees. For off-plan property, this fee is typically paid when the purchase is registered, and the timing and any developer subsidy vary by project, so ask your agent to confirm. Ready property also has trustee and developer NOC fees. At Marina Crest, buyers of off-plan property pay 0% commission because the developer pays it, and buyers of ready (resale) property pay 2%.

## Time, risks, resale and service charges: off-plan compared with ready property
Off-plan property typically takes one to several years to complete, and the handover date can be delayed, so you cannot live in it or earn rent until handover. Ready property can be inspected before you buy and used right away. Ready property is already charged service charges, while off-plan service charges usually start after handover. Reselling an off-plan unit before handover is sometimes possible, but it typically needs developer approval, may require a minimum amount to have been paid, and varies by developer.

## Who each option typically suits and questions to ask before choosing
Off-plan often appeals to buyers who can wait and prefer staged payments. Ready property often suits buyers who want to move in or rent out quickly. Neither is better for everyone, and Marina Crest does not guarantee price growth or rental returns for either. Before choosing, ask:

* Is the project registered, and which escrow account receives payments?
* What is the payment schedule and the expected handover date?
* What happens if handover is delayed?
* What are the expected service charges?
* For ready property, what condition is it in, and does the seller have any outstanding mortgage?
